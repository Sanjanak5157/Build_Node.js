Yes. Let's take your **Mediunify Doctor frontend** example.

Suppose the doctor clicks **"Patients"** and wants to see a patient's details.

### What happens?

```text
Doctor Frontend
      ↓
"View Patient"
      ↓
Route
      ↓
Controller
      ↓
Service
      ↓
Database
      ↓
Service
      ↓
Controller
      ↓
Frontend
```

### In very simple words

**1. Frontend**

Doctor clicks:

> View Patient

The frontend sends a request:

```text
GET /api/patients/123
```

**2. Route**

The route receives:

```text
/api/patients/123
```

and sends it to the correct controller.

**3. Controller**

The controller says:

> "I need the details of patient 123."

It calls the service.

**4. Service**

The service gets the patient information from the database:

```text
Name
Age
Phone
Medical history
Vitals
Previous consultations
Prescriptions
```

**5. Database**

The database finds patient `123` and sends the information back.

**6. Backend sends response**

The backend sends the patient details to the frontend, usually as JSON:

```json
{
  "name": "Rahul",
  "age": 32,
  "medicalHistory": "Diabetes",
  "phone": "XXXXXXXXXX"
}
```

**7. Frontend**

The React Native/Expo app receives this data and displays:

```text
Patient Details

Name: Rahul
Age: 32
Medical History: Diabetes
Phone: XXXXXXXXXX
```

### The easiest way to remember

```text
Frontend → asks
Route → directs
Controller → handles
Service → does the work
Database → gives the data
Backend → sends it back
Frontend → shows it
```

So **Controller and Service are not directly visible to the doctor**. They work behind the scenes to get the patient data and send it to your doctor app.
